How it works

  1. History. Every FBS game since 2014 with final scores and the consensus closing spread, total, and moneylines (the median across sportsbooks), from the open cfbfastR data.
  2. This season. Schedules, live scores, and DraftKings lines come from ESPN, including DraftKings' closing line for every finished game. Finished weeks feed the model, so it keeps learning as the season goes.
  3. Team stats. Each team has an Elo rating (adjusted for margin of victory, home field, and FBS vs. FCS), rolling points scored and allowed, and how often they cover and go over. Recent games count most, and ratings regress toward average each offseason.
  4. Learning week to week. Three logistic-regression models (win, cover, over) predict each week using only earlier games, see the results, and update before the next week.
  5. Every sportsbook. The model's view becomes an expected margin and total, which prices each book's own line and odds, including half-point differences and pushes. A bet is recommended at the book where its expected value is highest, if that clears your minimum edge.

Where the lines come from

  • DraftKings through ESPN, with opening lines (refreshed every 2 minutes).
  • DraftKings betting splits: the share of money and of bets on each side (every 5 minutes).
  • Hard Rock Bet, FanDuel, BetMGM, theScore Bet (formerly ESPN BET), BetRivers, and Bovada (plus Caesars and Fanatics when they post college lines) through The Odds API, refreshed every 5 hours during the season to stay within the free plan.

How accurate is it?

For education and entertainment. Closing college football lines are efficient, and no model reliably beats them. Check the track record before trusting any pick. Never bet more than you can afford to lose. Need help? Call 1-800-GAMBLER.
