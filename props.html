How it works

  1. Data. Every QB, RB, WR, and TE stat line from last season and this one, read from ESPN's box score for every game involving an FBS team. Finished weeks are stored once, so new weeks show up automatically. Positions come from team rosters (cfbfastR); a player missing from them gets one from his stats.
  2. Projection. For each player and stat, a weighted average of his games (recent games count most, last season counts about a third), pulled slightly toward the position average. It's nudged for the opponent's defense against that position and for the team's implied points from the betting line, which matters in college, where 50-point favorites and 20-point underdogs share a schedule.
  3. Learning week to week. The model walks through every week in order. It projects that week, sees the results, and learns. It corrects projections that run high or low for each kind of player, how spread out results are, and how often results land above or below each line. Each new week of games updates all three.
  4. Probability. The projection becomes a full range of outcomes (a gamma curve for yards, a normal curve for QB passing volume, and count distributions for catches, carries, and touchdowns). That range gives the chance of going over or under any line, including pushes on whole-number lines.
  5. Edge. Same rule as the game lines: expected value = win chance × payout − loss chance. A side is suggested when that's at least your minimum edge.

How accurate is it?

Tested walk-forward on the 2025 season and the first nine weeks of 2026, where every projection used only earlier games: projections were about 2–4% more accurate than a player's simple last-8-game average. At the model's fair line, overs landed about 53% of the time instead of 50%, so it leans slightly low on yardage and catches. College stat lines swing more than the NFL's (blowouts pull starters, depth charts change weekly, and box scores leave out players with no stats), so treat every number as a starting point. The report card above shows how it's doing this season.
